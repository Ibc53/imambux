Doctoral Research

Willingness of Dairy Farmers to Adopt Recommended Dairy Farming Practices in Tharparkar District, Sindh, Pakistan

My PhD research at Universiti Putra Malaysia investigates how smallholder dairy farmers evaluate and respond to recommended dairy farming practices in an arid and climate-vulnerable livestock production system.

The study examines the roles of farmer knowledge, attitudes, communication sources, perceived attributes of agricultural innovations, socio-economic characteristics, and economic, informational, and behavioural barriers in shaping willingness to adopt improved dairy practices.

The research is grounded in the Diffusion of Innovations (DOI), Technology Acceptance Model (TAM), and Theory of Planned Behaviour (TPB), allowing adoption decisions to be understood as a combination of knowledge, perception, behavioural readiness, and real-world enabling conditions.

Field data were collected from 430 valid dairy farmers across all seven talukas and 168 Dehs of Tharparkar District through structured face-to-face interviews.

My doctoral corrections have been submitted to the examination committee, and the PhD is currently in the final completion stage.

Current Research Themes

Farmer Technology Adoption & Behaviour

I investigate why farmers become willing—or remain unable—to adopt agricultural innovations, with particular interest in knowledge, attitudes, perceived usefulness, ease of use, compatibility, behavioural control, and adoption readiness.

Agricultural Extension & Advisory Systems

My work examines how extension systems can move beyond one-way information delivery toward farmer-centred advisory approaches that connect technical knowledge with services, training, inputs, demonstrations, and practical implementation support.

Innovation Diffusion & Communication Channels

I study how extension agents, interpersonal networks, mobile phones, digital platforms, mass media, demonstrations, and community-based communication channels influence farmer learning and agricultural technology diffusion.

Adoption Barriers & Enabling Conditions

A major part of my research focuses on informational, economic, behavioural, institutional, and service-access barriers that prevent willingness from becoming actual implementation.

Dairy & Livestock Production Systems

My doctoral work uses smallholder dairy farming as an applied setting for examining innovation adoption, animal-health practices, farm management, farmer decision-making, livelihood resilience, and extension support.

Climate-Smart & Sustainable Agriculture

I am increasingly interested in adoption of climate-smart practices, sustainable livestock systems, resource-efficient farming, climate-risk communication, and strategies that strengthen resilience among resource-constrained farming households.

Digital Agricultural Extension

My emerging research agenda explores digital advisory systems, mobile-based extension, artificial intelligence-supported agricultural communication, farmer trust in digital information, and technology acceptance in rural communities.

Agricultural Knowledge & Innovation Systems

I am interested in Agricultural Knowledge and Innovation Systems (AKIS), particularly the relationships among farmers, researchers, extension organizations, universities, government agencies, private-sector actors, and rural institutions.

International & Comparative Research

MIDORI Rice Practices – Japan

During my academic mobility at Kyushu University, I developed a stakeholder-focused study examining farmer awareness and perceptions of sustainable MIDORI rice practices in Itoshima, Japan.

The project connected agricultural sustainability with innovation diffusion, farmer perceptions, communication, and adoption readiness.

Comparative Agricultural Innovation

My international research interests include comparing agricultural technology adoption and advisory systems across developed and developing-country contexts, particularly Japan, South Asia, Southeast Asia, and other Global South farming systems.

Future Research Direction

I aim to extend my research from willingness and intention toward actual adoption, implementation intensity, sustained practice use, behavioural interventions, digital advisory systems, and comparative agricultural policy research.

Research Insights & Applied Contribution

Knowledge & Attitudes

My research demonstrates that farmers’ knowledge and attitudes are important components of adoption readiness, but their influence must be understood alongside structural and institutional conditions.

Innovation Attributes

Farmers are more likely to consider agricultural practices when innovations are perceived as compatible, useful, manageable, observable, and appropriate to their farming circumstances.

Information & Economic Constraints

My barriers research highlights that low adoption readiness should not automatically be interpreted as farmer resistance. Information gaps, affordability, input availability, veterinary services, and institutional support may significantly constrain farmers who are otherwise willing to improve their practices.

Enabling-Condition Extension

This work supports an extension approach that combines practical information with affordable implementation pathways, service access, demonstrations, local-language communication, and farmer-centred advisory support.
