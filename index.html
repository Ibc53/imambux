How I Create Impact

My academic and professional activities connect research evidence with agricultural education, extension practice, farmer learning, and sustainable rural development.

Research & Innovation

I investigate farmer knowledge, attitudes, technology acceptance, communication channels, and the economic and institutional factors shaping agricultural innovation adoption.

My work draws on Diffusion of Innovations, the Technology Acceptance Model, and the Theory of Planned Behavior to inform evidence-based extension strategies.

Featured Doctoral Research

PhD Research — Pakistan

Willingness of Dairy Farmers to Adopt Recommended Dairy Farming Practices in Tharparkar District, Sindh, Pakistan

My doctoral research examined the factors influencing dairy farmers' willingness to adopt recommended farming practices. The study analyzed data from 430 dairy farmers across seven talukas of Tharparkar District.

The integrated regression model explained approximately 65.7% of the variation in farmers' willingness to adopt recommended dairy farming practices.

International Research — Japan

Bridging the Diffusion Gap: MIDORI Rice Practices in Itoshima

During my academic exchange at Kyushu University, Japan (October 2025–March 2026), I investigated farmer awareness, perceptions, and adoption-related challenges associated with sustainable rice farming practices.

This research contributed to my international perspective on agricultural innovation, farmer learning, and sustainability-oriented extension.

From Willingness to Adoption

My research emphasizes the distinction between farmers' stated willingness to adopt agricultural innovations and their actual adoption behavior.

I seek to translate behavioral research into practical extension interventions that address information gaps, affordability, advisory support, institutional access, and local farming conditions.
